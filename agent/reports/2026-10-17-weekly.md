## Research

### Q: Does the G2 paper strategy's 2-minute open-auction wait materially erode its edge?

**Motivation.** G2 paper logs both `bps_at_0930_open` (the gap size at auction open) and
`bps_at_0932_entry` (at actual entry). The paper mean is −1.116 bps — just below the +5 bps bar.
If the 2-minute delay costs meaningful bps, earlier entries would look more attractive.

**Method.** For each of the 58 rows in `g2_paper_ledger.csv` with both columns populated (3 rows
excluded: GRMN Oct 12, LVS Oct 12, PVH Oct 16), I computed the raw delta:
δ = bps_at_0932_entry − bps_at_0930_open.

This measures how much the bps signal changed in those 2 minutes. For a gap-fade strategy, a
negative δ on a short (or positive δ on a buy against a down-gap) generally means the trade entered
at a worse level than auction open provided.

**Results — daily mean δ:**

| Day | Trades | Mean δ (bps) |
|---|---|---|
| Oct 9 | 10 | −13.4 |
| Oct 12 | 8 | +13.3 |
| Oct 13 | 10 | −13.9 |
| Oct 14 | 10 | −7.4 |
| Oct 15 | 10 | −1.3 |
| Oct 16 | 9 | −12.7 |
| **6-day mean** | **57** | **−5.9** |

The gap narrows by about 5–6 bps on average during the 2-minute wait. Oct 12 is the only day where
waiting improved the entry (+13.3 bps), driven by a few names where the gap widened at open.
High trade-level variance (range roughly −125 to +140 bps per trade) means the mean is noisy.

**Interpretation for G2 paper.** The 6-day paper mean is −1.116 bps at entry. If open-auction
entries had been used instead, the estimated mean would be ≈ −1.116 + 5.9 = **+4.8 bps** — still
below the +5 bps bar, but barely. This is not a reliable conclusion at n=58 pairs and 6 days. The
variance of the daily δ (std dev ≈ 11 bps/day) swamps the signal.

**Verdict: inconclusive.** The delay costs are non-trivial but cannot be distinguished from noise at
this sample size. The edge question for G2 is not primarily about 2-minute slippage — it is whether
the signal itself has positive expectancy, which 6 days cannot answer. No action warranted.
